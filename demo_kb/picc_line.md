# PICC line (peripherally inserted central catheter)

A PICC is a thin, soft tube placed into a vein in the arm and advanced so the tip sits in a large central vein. Image guidance helps the IR team place it safely.

Why a child might need a PICC:

- Longer courses of intravenous antibiotics or nutrition
- Frequent blood tests or medicines that irritate small veins
- Treatment that would otherwise need many needle sticks

What families often ask:

- Placement is usually done under sedation or anaesthesia in children
- The arm is kept clean and dry; the dressing is changed by trained staff
- Swimming and rough play are usually restricted until the team says otherwise
- Redness, swelling, leaking, or fever should be reported promptly

A PICC is not a diagnosis or a treatment plan by itself. The child's doctors decide how long it stays in and when it can come out.
