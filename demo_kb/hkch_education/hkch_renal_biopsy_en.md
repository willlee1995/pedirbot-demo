# Kidney biopsy

Source: Hong Kong Children's Hospital, Department of Radiology. Official IR Clinic nursing education leaflet.

Department of Radiology

Kidney Biopsy

1. What is a kidney biopsy?

A kidney biopsy uses a biopsy needle to remove small cylinders (“cores”) of tissue of kidney tissue, either from your child’s own kidneys (native) or from a transplanted kidney (graft), so they can be examined under a microscope.

Ultrasound is usually used to locate the kidney and guide the needle into the correct area, which improves safety and accuracy.

2. Before the procedure

- Fasting: Follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthetist if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned. If only local anaesthesia is being planned, your child should have no food, milk, or formula for 4 hours before the procedure.
- Anaesthetic assessment: Attend the pre anaesthesia clinic if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, as instructed by your healthcare team.
- Admission slip: Please bring the admission slip with you on the procedure day for admission.
- Blood tests: Perform blood tests as instructed by your referring unit, usually within 2 weeks before the procedure.
- Fever or upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) symptoms: Inform the staff if your child develops a fever or any URTI symptoms (such as cough or runny nose) within one week before the procedure; the procedure may need to be delayed.
- Medications: Inform the doctor if your child takes blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines (for example, aspirin, clopidogrel)
- Previous contrast reaction: Inform the doctor if your child has had any previous reaction to contrast media, such as rash, swelling, or breathing difficulty.
- Accompany your child: Please accompany your child on the procedure day and stay with them until they arrive at the examination room.
- High blood pressure: Good blood pressure control is important before the procedure, as high blood pressure can increase bleeding risk.

3. During the procedure

- Position: For native kidney biopsy, your child usually lies on their belly with a pillow under the abdomen so the back is slightly arched; for a kidney graft in the lower abdomen, your child lies on their back.
- Anaesthesia/sedation: Local anaesthetic is injected into the skin and deeper tissues so that the area becomes numb. Most children also receive sedation or general anaesthesia to keep them remain still and comfortable. If sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, the anaesthetist will help put your child to sleep.
- Biopsy: Under ultrasound guidance, the doctor inserts the biopsy needle into the kidney and takes two or more small cores of tissue.
- Duration: The procedure itself takes around 30 minutes, but preparation and recovery mean your child will be in the Department of Radiology for 1-2 hours.

4. After the procedure and home care

- Observation: Your child will be observed on the hospital ward for at least 6 hours after the biopsy to ensure there is no significant bleeding.
- Bed rest: Your child must lie flat in bed for at least 6 hours after the procedure. During this time, they may perform gentle activities and eat, but they should get up only to use the bedpan provided for toileting while in bed.
- Monitoring: Nurses regularly check blood pressure, pulse, temperature, pain level, and the biopsy site for bleeding. Urine is checked for blood; pink or slightly blood‑stained urine may be common in the first day, but it should gradually clear.
- Pressure dressing: A pressure dressing will be applied after the biopsy. Keep the pressure dressing in place as instructed by clinical team (usually for 24 hours).
- Local care: After the pressure dressing is removed (usually after 24 hours), a small plaster may be placed on the needle site; keep the area clean and dry for at least 48 hours after the dressing is removed or as instructed.
- Showering and bathing: Your child may shower the day after the biopsy, but should not soak in a bath, hot tub, or swimming pool for at least 48 hours.

- Pain: Mild back or abdominal discomfort near the biopsy site is common and usually relieved with painkillers. Avoid aspirin or ibuprofen if advised, as they can increase bleeding.
- Physical activity: Your child should rest in bed as much as possible for the first 24 hours after the procedure. For the next 2 weeks, avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, running, climbing, gym classes, contact sports (for example, football, soccer, basketball, gymnastics), bicycle riding, horseback riding, or any activity that could hit the stomach or back.
- Return to school/normal life: Most children can return to school or work the day after the biopsy, unless otherwise instructed. They should not participate in physical education class for 2 to 4 weeks.

5. When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care if:

- Your child has bright‑red or heavily blood‑stained urine, blood clots in the urine, or urine that stays very dark and does not gradually clear.
- There is new or worsening pain in the back, side, or biopsy area, especially with dizziness, paleness, or weakness.
- There is bleeding from the biopsy site, or the area becomes very swollen or firm.
- There are signs of infection, such as redness spreading from the wound, warmth, pus, or fever.

6. Results

The kidney tissue is examined under a microscope by a pathologist.

Your child’s kidney team will review the results with you and explain how the biopsy results can guide further management plan.
