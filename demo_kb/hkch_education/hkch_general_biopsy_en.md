# Core needle biopsy (general sites)

Source: Hong Kong Children's Hospital, Department of Radiology. Official IR Clinic nursing education leaflet.

Core Needle Biopsy (General Sites)

1. What is a core needle biopsy?

A core needle biopsy uses a biopsy needle to remove small cylinders of tissue from an area of concern seen on imaging so they can be examined under a microscope.\
Ultrasound is used to guide the needle into the correct area, which improves safety and accuracy.

2. Before the procedure

- Fasting: Follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthetist if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned. If only local anaesthesia is planned, your child should have no food, milk, or formula for 4 hours before the procedure.
- Anaesthetic assessment: Attend the pre-anaesthesia clinic if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, as instructed by your healthcare team.
- Admission slip: Please bring the admission slip with you on the procedure day.
- Blood tests: Have blood tests done as instructed by your referring unit, usually within 2 weeks before the procedure.
- Fever or URTI symptoms: Inform the staff if your child develops a fever or any upper respiratory tract infection symptoms, such as cough or runny nose, within 1 week before the procedure. The procedure may need to be delayed.
- Medicines: Inform the doctor if your child takes blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines, for example aspirin or clopidogrel.
- Accompany your child: Please accompany your child on the procedure day and stay with them until they arrive at the examination room.

3. During the procedure

- Position: It depends on the location of the biopsy site. The biopsy site will be exposed and disinfected.
- Anaesthesia/sedation: Local anaesthetic is injected into the skin and deeper tissues so that the area becomes numb. If sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, the anaesthetist will manage this.
- Biopsy: Using ultrasound as a guide, the doctor inserts the biopsy needle into the area and takes several small cores of tissue; a clicking sound may be heard with some devices.
- Duration: The procedure itself usually takes less than 30 minutes once everything is prepared.

4. After the procedure and home care

- Observation: Your child will be observed on the hospital ward for at least 4 hours after the biopsy to ensure there is no significant bleeding.
- Monitoring: Nurses regularly check blood pressure, pulse, temperature, pain level, and the biopsy site for bleeding.
- Local care: After the pressure dressing is removed (usually after 24 hours), a small plaster may be placed on the needle site; keep the area clean and dry for at least 48 hours after the dressing is removed or as instructed.
- Pain: Mild discomfort near the biopsy site is common and usually relieved with painkillers. Avoid aspirin or ibuprofen if advised, as they can increase bleeding.
- Return to school/normal life: Most children can return to school or work the day after the biopsy, unless otherwise instructed.

5. When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care if:

- The biopsy site bleeds and does not stop after firm pressure.
- There is increasing swelling, significant bruising, or worsening pain.
- There are signs of infection, such as redness spreading from the wound, warmth, pus, or fever.

6. Results

The tissue samples are examined in the laboratory by pathologists.\
Your child’s doctor will review the results with you and explain how the biopsy results can guide further management plan.
