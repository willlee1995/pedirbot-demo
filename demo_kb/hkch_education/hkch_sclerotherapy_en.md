# Sclerotherapy

Source: Hong Kong Children's Hospital, Department of Radiology. Official IR Clinic nursing education leaflet.

Sclerotherapy

1. What is sclerotherapy?

Sclerotherapy is a minimally invasive procedure used to treat certain vascular malformations, such as venous or lymphatic malformations. A medicine called a sclerosant is injected into the abnormal blood or lymph vessels. This irritates the vessel lining and causes the abnormal vessels to shrink or close.

Ultrasound, X-ray, or other imaging may be used to guide the needle or catheter into the treatment area. The procedure may need to be repeated, depending on the size, location, and response of the malformation.

2. Before the procedure

- Fasting: Follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthetist if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned. If only local anaesthesia is being planned, your child should have no food, milk, or formula for 4 hours before the procedure.
- Anaesthetic assessment: Attend the pre anaesthesia clinic if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, as instructed by your healthcare team.
- Admission slip: Please bring the admission slip with you on the procedure day for admission.
- Blood tests: Perform blood tests as instructed by your referring unit, usually within 2 weeks before the procedure.
- Fever or upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) symptoms: Inform the staff if your child develops a fever or any URTI symptoms (such as cough or runny nose) within one week before the procedure; the procedure may need to be delayed.
- Medications: Inform the doctor if your child takes blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines (for example, aspirin, clopidogrel)
- Previous contrast reaction: Inform the doctor if your child has had any previous reaction to contrast media, such as rash, swelling, or breathing difficulty. Steroid premedication may be needed.
- Accompany your child: Please accompany your child on the procedure day and stay with them until they arrive at the examination room.

3. During the procedure

- Position: Your child will lie on the examination table in a position that allows the doctor to access the malformation safely. The treatment area will be exposed and disinfected.
- Anaesthesia/sedation: Most children receive sedation or general anaesthesia so they are asleep and comfortable during the procedure.
- Imaging guidance: Ultrasound, X-ray, or other imaging may be used to guide the needle or catheter into the abnormal vessels.
- Sclerotherapy: The doctor injects the sclerosant into the malformation.
- Duration: The procedure may take several hours, depending on the location and extent of the malformation and whether more than one area is treated.
- After the injection: A dressing or compression bandage may be applied to the treated area.

4. After the procedure and home care

- Observation: Your child will be monitored in the recovery area until they are awake and comfortable.
- Pain and swelling: Pain, swelling, bruising, and tightness around the treated area are common for the first few days. Swelling may take 1 to 2 weeks to improve, while bruising may last several weeks. When the local anaesthetic begins to wear off, the area may feel bruised and sore. If your child has been prescribed pain medicine, follow the instructions provided.
- Dressing and bandage: Keep the dressing or bandage in place for the period advised by your team, usually 24 to 48 hours. Keep it clean and dry.

- Compression garment: If a compression garment has been prescribed, your child should resume wearing it when instructed by the treating doctor. The timing may vary depending on the treated area and swelling.
- Eating and drinking: Once your child is fully awake and feels well after the anaesthetic, they may return to their usual diet. Encourage plenty of fluids for the first 48 hours.
- Activity: Your child should rest the treated limb for the first 48 hours where possible. After that, encourage gentle movement to prevent stiffness. Normal activities can usually be resumed gradually as your child feels comfortable.
- School and sports: Your child may return to school when they feel well enough. Avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting, and vigorous sports until advised by the treating team.

5. When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care if your child develops:

- Severe or increasing pain that is not relieved by the recommended pain medicine.
- Rapidly increasing swelling or tightness around the treated area.
- Skin discolouration, blistering, skin breakdown, or a new open sore over the treated area.
- Numbness, pins and needles, weakness, or difficulty moving the treated limb or body part.
- Fever, spreading redness, warmth, pus, or other signs of infection.
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain. These complications are rare but require urgent assessment.

6. Follow-up

- Your child’s doctor will arrange follow-up to assess the response to treatment and check the treated area. Further imaging may be arranged if needed.
- Sclerotherapy may need to be repeated because vascular malformations can require more than one treatment session.
