# Breast biopsy

Source: Hong Kong Children's Hospital, Department of Radiology. Official IR Clinic nursing education leaflet.

Breast Core Needle Biopsy

1. What is a breast core needle biopsy?Breast procedure

A breast core needle biopsy uses a biopsy needle to remove small cylinders (“cores”) of tissue from a breast lump or abnormal area seen on imaging so they can be examined under a microscope.\
Ultrasound is used to guide the needle into the correct area, which improves safety and accuracy.

2. Before the procedure

- Fasting: Follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthetist if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned. If only local anaesthesia is being planned, your child should have no food, milk, or formula for 4 hours before the procedure.
- Anaesthetic assessment: Attend the pre anaesthesia clinic if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, as instructed by your healthcare team.
- Admission slip: Please bring the admission slip with you on the procedure day for admission.
- Blood tests: Perform blood tests as instructed by your referring unit, usually within 2 weeks before the procedure.
- Fever or upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) symptoms: Inform the staff if your child develops a fever or any URTI symptoms (such as cough or runny nose) within one week before the procedure; the procedure may need to be delayed.
- Medications: Inform the doctor if your child takes blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines (for example, aspirin, clopidogrel)
- Accompany your child: Please accompany your child on the procedure day and stay with them until they arrive at the examination room.

3. During the procedureBreast core puncture

- Position: Your child lies on their back on the examination table, and the breast and skin are cleaned with antiseptic.
- Anaesthesia/sedation: Local anaesthetic is injected into the skin and deeper tissues so that the area becomes numb. If sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, the anaesthetist will manage this.
- Biopsy: Using ultrasound as a guide, the doctor inserts the biopsy needle into the area and takes several small cores of tissue; a clicking sound may be heard with some devices.
- Duration: The procedure itself usually takes within 30 minutes once everything is prepared.

4. After the procedure and home care

- Observation: Your child will be observed for a short period to ensure there is no significant bleeding or swelling.
- Local care: A dressing and sometimes adhesive strips (for example, Steristrips) are applied; keep the area clean and dry for the first 48 hours or as instructed. Do not rub the area.
- Pressure for bleeding: Apply firm pressure to the puncture site until bleeding has stopped. Pressure may be applied for longer in children with higher bleeding risk or if oozing continues.
- Support: Wearing a comfortable, supportive bra day and night for 24 hours can reduce discomfort and bruising.
- Pain: When the local anaesthetic begins to wear off, the area may feel bruised and sore. Light painkillers are usually sufficient; avoid aspirin or ibuprofen if advised, as they can increase bleeding.
- Bruising: Bruising can occur after the biopsy and may take a few weeks to resolve. Occasionally, there may be some bleeding inside the breast causing a larger bruise or swelling (hematoma), which usually resolves in a few weeks.
- Activity: Light activities can be continued as normal. Avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting, and vigorous exercise for 24 to 48 hours after the procedure.

5. When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care if:

- The biopsy site begins to bleed and does not stop after firm pressure for 10 minutes.
- There is increasing swelling, significant bruising, or pain that is getting worse.
- There are signs of infection, such as redness spreading from the wound, warmth, pus, or fever.

6. Results

The tissue samples are examined in the laboratory by pathologists.\
Your child’s doctor will discuss the findings and next steps with you, which may include further imaging, surgery, or followup.
