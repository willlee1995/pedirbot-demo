# Thyroid fine-needle aspiration

Source: Hong Kong Children's Hospital, Department of Radiology. Official IR Clinic nursing education leaflet.

Thyroid Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA)

1. What is thyroid FNA?

-  uses a very thin needle to take a small sample of cells from a thyroid nodule in the neck so they can be examined under a microscope.
- Ultrasound is used to guide the needle into the correct part of the nodule, which improves safety and accuracy.

2. Before the procedure

- Fasting: Follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthetist if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned. If only local anaesthesia is being planned, your child should have no food, milk, or formula for 4 hours before the procedure.
- Anaesthetic assessment: Attend the pre anaesthesia clinic if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, as instructed by your healthcare team.
- Admission slip: Please bring the admission slip with you on the procedure day for admission.
- Blood tests: Perform blood tests as instructed by your referring unit, usually within 2 weeks before the procedure.
- Fever or upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) symptoms: Inform the staff if your child develops a fever or any URTI symptoms (such as cough or runny nose) within one week before the procedure; the procedure may need to be delayed.
- Medications: Inform the doctor if your child takes blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines (for example, aspirin, clopidogrel)
- Accompany your child: Please accompany your child on the procedure day and stay with them until they arrive at the examination room.

3. During the procedure

- Position: Your child lies on the back with the neck slightly extended; a pillow or small roll may be placed under the shoulders.
- Anaesthesia/sedation: The skin over the thyroid is cleaned, and local anaesthetic is injected to numb the area. If sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, the anaesthetist will help put your child to sleep.
- Sampling: Under ultrasound guidance, the doctor inserts a fine needle into the nodule and gently draws out cells; several samples may be taken. Your child may feel some moving sensation around the neck region.
- Duration: The procedure itself usually takes within 30 minutes to 1hour.

4. After the procedure and home care

- Observation: Your child will be observed for a short period to ensure there is no significant bleeding, neck swelling or breathing difficulty.
- Local care: A small plaster is placed on the needle site; keep it dry for the first 24 hours or as instructed. Pressure should be applied until bleeding stops, then a small dressing/plaster is applied.
- Pain: Your child may experience mild neck discomfort or bruising,
- Activity: Light activities are usually allowed the same day; avoid vigorous neck movements, contact sports, or heavy exercise for 24–48 hours.

5. When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care if:

- There is increasing neck swelling, significant bruising, or pain that is getting worse.
- Your child has difficulty breathing, swallowing, or speaking, or develops noisy breathing.
- There is continuous bleeding from the puncture site that does not stop with gentle pressure.

6. Results

- The sample is sent to the laboratory for analysis.
- Your child’s doctor will explain the results and discuss any further treatment or followup needed.
