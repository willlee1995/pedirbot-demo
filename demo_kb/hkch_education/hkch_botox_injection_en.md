# Botulinum toxin (Botox) injection

Source: Hong Kong Children's Hospital, Department of Radiology. Official IR Clinic nursing education leaflet.

Ultrasound-Guided Botox Injection of the Salivary Glands

1. What is this procedure?Salivary glands map

Ultrasound-guided Botox injection of the salivary glands is a procedure used to reduce drooling by injecting a small amount of botulinum toxin into the salivary glands. Ultrasound is used to guide the needle into the correct part of the gland, which improves accuracy and safety.

2. Before the procedure

- Fasting: Follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthetist if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned. If only local anaesthesia is being planned, your child should have no food, milk, or formula for 4 hours before the procedure.
- Anaesthetic assessment: Attend the pre anaesthesia clinic if sedation or general anaesthesia is planned, as instructed by your healthcare team.
- Admission slip: Please bring the admission slip with you on the procedure day for admission.
- Blood tests: Perform blood tests as instructed by your referring unit, usually within 2 weeks before the procedure.
- Fever or upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) symptoms: Inform the staff if your child develops a fever or any URTI symptoms (such as cough or runny nose) within one week before the procedure; the procedure may need to be delayed.
- Medications: Inform the doctor if your child takes blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines (for example, aspirin, clopidogrel)
- Accompany your child: Please accompany your child on the procedure day and stay with them until they arrive at the examination room.

3. During the procedureBotox injection

- Position: Your child will lie on the examination table, usually on their back, while the doctor uses ultrasound to locate the salivary gland.
- Anaesthesia/sedation: Younger children or children who may not cooperate well usually receive sedation or general anaesthesia. Some older children may be awake with local anaesthetic or topical numbing cream, depending on the team’s plan.
- Injection: Using ultrasound guidance, the doctor inserts a small needle directly into the salivary gland and injects a small amount of botulinum toxin.
- Duration: The procedure itself usually takes within 30 minutes once everything is prepared.

4. After the procedure and home care

- Observation: Your child will be monitored in recovery for a short period to ensure there is no bleeding or other immediate problem.
- Local care: Sometimes a small bandage is applied to the injection site. If a bandage is used, keep it clean and dry as instructed by your team.
- Pain: Mild soreness or bruising at the injection sites is common. This is usually not severe and can be managed with light painkillers.
- Eating and drinking: Once your child is fully awake and feels well, they may return to their usual diet and should drink plenty of fluids for the first 48 hours.
- Activity: Most children can resume their regular activities as soon as they feel able to do so.
- Effect of treatment: The Botox usually starts to work after a few weeks, and the full effect may take 2 to 3 weeks. The result often lasts 3 to 6 months.

5. When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care if your child develops:

- Difficulty breathing, take your child to the nearest Emergency Department right away.
- Difficulty swallowing or jaw weakness.
- Redness, swelling, or increasing pain at the injection site.
- Fever or a general feeling of being unwell after the procedure.
- Voice changes, drooping eyelids, vision changes, or severe weakness.

6. Follow-up

Your child’s doctor will explain what to expect and arrange follow-up as needed.
